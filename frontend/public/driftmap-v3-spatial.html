01How it works

From samples to ranked findings in three steps

No custom DSL or manual JSON Schema authoring. Drop in raw response bodies or OpenAPI documents and let the evidence guide the review.

Step 1

Add samples

Paste raw JSON payloads or import OpenAPI 3.0 specs in JSON or YAML, up to 2 MB. Group them into baseline and candidate sets in a local workspace.

baseline_orders_v1.json3 samples
candidate_orders_v2.yamlOpenAPI 3.0
Step 2

Infer the contract

Every sample merges into a normalized path tree that records observed types, nullability, and whether each field was always present.

/customer/emailstring · req (3/3)
/items/*/skustring|number · req
Step 3

Review classified drift

Each difference is scored by compatibility rules and surfaced with before and after evidence you can filter and export.

Breaking (2) Warning (1) Safe (1)

Semantic drift classification

Breaking changes are separated from warnings, safe additions, and informational notes, so the riskiest findings rise to the top.

breaking Field removed, type narrowed
warning Became nullable, required to optional
safe Optional field added
info Unchanged baseline contract

04Limitations

What it doesn’t do yet

DriftMap is built for fast, honest contract triage. Know the boundaries of sample-based inference before you add it to a release checklist.

Inference, not a schema validator

Rare fields missing from your samples won’t appear in the inferred contract.

No fuzzy rename matching

A renamed field appears as one removal plus one addition.

OpenAPI 3.0 only

JSON and YAML. OpenAPI 3.1 and Swagger 2.0 are not parsed, and external $ref URLs are rejected.

2 MB import ceiling

Per payload or specification document, to keep in-browser parsing responsive.

Heuristic guidance

Classifications are compatibility guidance, not a guarantee of runtime safety.

Browser-scoped persistence

Local data lives in your browser’s IndexedDB unless you sign in and sync.
